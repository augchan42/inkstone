# Hexagram Dossiers — Shaughnessy's Directly-Analyzed Hexagrams

Line-level reference for the hexagrams Shaughnessy treats in *The Composition of the Zhouyi* (1983), Chs. 3–4. Use this when reading a **specific line** of a listed hexagram, so that the `[Shaughnessy's own analysis]` tag holds *down to the line position*, not just the theme.

**Accuracy note.** Line statements below are from the dissertation's own transcription/translation (sibling repo `chinese-classics-reference/shaugnessy/text/`). They reflect Shaughnessy's *emended/reconstructed* readings, which sometimes differ from the received text and from Wilhelm. When exact characters matter, verify against `shaugnessy/text/04-chapter-three-*.md` and `05-chapter-four-*.md`. If a hexagram you need is listed as **thematic only**, you do **not** have his line placement — read the line as `[method extrapolated]` even though the hexagram is in his corpus.

## 乾 Qian (1) — Structural Paradigm · the Dragon constellation across the growing year
- 1/1 潛龍勿用 — "Submerged dragon; do not use." (Winter solstice; dragon invisible below the horizon.)
- 1/2 見龍在田，利見大人 — "See the dragon in the fields." (Early March; horns rise over the eastern horizon.)
- 1/3 君子終日乾乾，夕惕若厲，无咎 — "The lordling throughout the day is vigorous; in the evening fearful as if in danger; no harm." (Transitional.)
- 1/4 或躍在淵，无咎 — "And now jumping in the depths; no harm." (Late April–May.)
- 1/5 飛龍在天，利見大人 — "Flying dragon in the skies." (Summer solstice; whole dragon arrayed across the sky.)
- 1/6 亢龍有悔 — "Necked dragon; there are problems." (Autumn; the 'Neck' star sinks toward the western horizon — same word *gang* 亢 as the star.)
- 1/7 (用九) 見群龍无首，吉 — "See the flock of dragons without heads; auspicious." (Final descent.)

## 坤 Kun (2) — Structural Paradigm · the agricultural autumn→winter (complement to Qian)
- 2/1 履霜，堅冰至 — "Treading on frost; the solid ice is coming." (9th month.)
- 2/2 直方…无不利 — "Inspecting the borderland… nothing not beneficial." (Post-harvest inspection.)
- 2/3 含章可貞，或從王事，无成有終 — "Containing a pattern; one may divine; attend to royal affairs; without completion but with an end." (Readiness.)
- 2/4 括囊，无咎无譽 — "Tying the sack; no harm but no praise." (Storing the harvest.)
- 2/5 黃裳，元吉 — "Yellow skirts; primary auspiciousness." (Harvest celebration.)
- 2/6 龍戰于野，其血玄黃，利永貞 — "The dragon fights in the wilds, his blood black and yellow; beneficial to perform a permanent divination." (Winter cosmic reversal.)

## 需 Xu (5) — Structural exemplar · one word stepped through changing referents
- 5/1 需于郊 — "Xu in the suburbs"
- 5/2 需于沙 — "Xu in the sand"
- 5/3 需于泥 — "Xu in the mud"
- 5/4 需于血 — "Xu in the blood"
- 5/5 需于酒食 — "Xu in the wine and food"
(Shaughnessy's flagship illustration that the line statements are *systematically differentiated* by a conscious editor.)

## 師 Shi (7) — Developed Narrative · a full military campaign
- 7/1 師出以律，否臧，凶 — "The troops go out in ranks; it is not good; inauspicious." (Mobilization / scene-setting.)
- 7/2 在師中，王三錫命，吉无咎 — "In the midst of the troops the king thrice bestows commands; auspicious, no harm." (Tactical orders.)
- 7/3 師或輿尸，凶 — "The troops now carting corpses; inauspicious." (Battle, casualties.)
- 7/4 師左次，无咎 — "The troops encamp on the left; no harm." (Garrison.)
- 7/5 田有禽，利執言，无咎；貞凶；長子帥師，弟子輿尸 — "In the fields there is a catch; beneficial to shackle captives… the elder son leads the troops, the younger carts the corpses." (Post-battle ritual hunt.)
- 7/6 大君有命，開國承家，小人勿用 — "The great lord has a mandate: open the state and maintain the family; the small man is not to be used." (Territorial reward.)

## 泰 Tai (11) / 否 Pi (12) — mirror-image pair
- Statements oppose: Tai 小往大來，吉亨 "the small go, the great come; auspicious; receipt" ↔ Pi 大往小來 "the great go, the small come."
- Identical first lines: 拔茅茹，以其彙 "Pluck the mao-grass and madder with its stem" (a *Shijing* image of marital separation).
- Shared 包 X "bundle X" topic: Tai 11/2 包荒; Pi 12/2 包承, 12/3 包羞.
- Tai 11/3 无平不陂，无往不復 — "There is no flat without a slope; no going without a return." (The pair's complementarity stated outright.)
- Thematic at most other line positions — verify before line-level `[own analysis]`.

## 同人 Tongren (13) / 大有 Dayou (14) — narrative pair · battle → sacrifice → reward
- 13/3 伏戎于莽，升其高陵，三歲不興 — "Lying enemy in the grass; ascend the high hillock; for three years they do not stir." (Engagement.)
- 13/4 乘其墉，弗克攻，吉 — "Astride the wall; it cannot be attacked; auspicious." (Victory.)
- 13/5 同人先號咷而後笑，大師克相遇 — "Gathering people: at first weeping, then laughing; the great troops can meet." (Fear → joy.)
- 13/6 同人于郊，无悔 — "Gathering people at the suburban altar; no problems." (Victory sacrifice at the *jiao*.)
- 14/3 公用亨于天子 — "The duke herewith makes an offering to the son of heaven."
- 14/6 自天祐之 — "From heaven, blessings to him." (Reward.)

## 隨 Sui (17) — Developed Narrative · pursuit, capture, royal sacrifice
- 17/1 官有渝，出門交有功，貞吉 — "There is a change in office; going out to make an exchange has merit; auspicious." (Scene.)
- 17/2 係小子，失丈夫 — "Tie the little boy but lose the man." (Pursuit begins.)
- 17/3 係丈夫，失小子；隨有求得，利居貞 — "Tie the man but lose the little boy; in the chase there is an attempt to capture; beneficial to perform a residential divination."
- 17/4 有孚在道，以明，何咎；隨有獲，貞凶 — "There is a captive in the road… in the chase there is a capture; divining: inauspicious."
- 17/5 孚于嘉，吉 — "A capture at Jia: auspicious." (Climax.)
- 17/6 拘係之，乃從維之，王用享于西山 — "Grasp and tie him, then guard him; the king herewith sacrifices on the western mountain." (Resolution; likely runaway bondservants.)

## 无妄 Wuwang (25) — Developed Omen · NOT "Innocence" but a plague
- 25/1 无妄往，吉 — "The *wuwang* goes: auspicious." (Plague may depart.)
- 25/2 不耕穫，不菑畬，則利有攸往 — "Not planting or harvesting… then beneficial to travel." (Agricultural devastation.)
- 25/3 无妄之災，或繫之牛，行人之得，邑人之災 — "The pestilence of the *wuwang*; someone fastens it to an ox; the traveller's gain is the townsman's pestilence." (**Scapegoat-ox ritual** — the affliction is loaded onto a bearer and led out of the village; carries 凶 force, harm falling on the one who did not earn it.)
- 25/5 无妄之疾，勿藥有喜 — "The *wuwang* sickness: no medicine but there is joy." (Resolved by ritual, not medicine — the hexagram's resolution, distinct from line 3's transfer.)
- 25/6 无妄行，有眚，无攸利 — "The *wuwang* moves about: there is harm; nothing beneficial." (Plague returns to full virulence.)

## 鼎 Ding (50) — Structural Paradigm · the cauldron, read against its parts
- 50/1 鼎顛趾，利出否；得妾以其子，无咎 — "The cauldron's upturned feet; beneficial to expel the bad; gain a consort and her child; no harm." (Legs.)
- 50/2 鼎有實…吉 — "The cauldron is full… auspicious." (Belly — ideal state.)
- 50/3 鼎耳革，其行塞 — "The cauldron's ears turned; its motion stopped." (Ears.)
- 50/4 鼎折足，覆公餗…凶 — "The cauldron's broken leg; overturns the duke's stew… inauspicious." (Structural failure; in Western Zhou symbolism could betoken executions.)
- 50/5 鼎黃耳金鉉，利貞 — "The cauldron's yellow ears and metal bar; beneficial to divine." (Ears + lifting bar.)
- 50/6 鼎玉鉉，大吉，无不利 — "The cauldron's jade bar; greatly auspicious; nothing not beneficial." (The bar that lifts it.)

## 震 Zhen (51) — Developed Omen · thunder as revivifying spring force (auspicious; opposite of Wuwang)
- Statement: 震來虩虩，笑言啞啞；震驚百里，不喪匕鬯 — "Thunder comes crackling-crackling; laughter and talk *ya-ya*; thunder startles a hundred *li*; do not lose the ladle and fragrant vine." (Spring fertility festival.)
- 51/1 震來虩虩 — "Thunder comes crackling-crackling." (*Xixi* carries the root 'to crack' — what thunder does to the frozen earth.)
- 51/2 震來厲 — "Thunder comes forcefully."
- 51/3 震蘇蘇 — "Thunder *su-su*" (regenerates; 蘇 = revive).
- 51/4 震遂泥 — "Thunder and then mud."
- 51/5 震往來厲 — "Thunder goes and comes forcefully."
- 51/6 震索索 — "Thunder *suo-suo*" (drawn-out).

## 艮 Gen (52) — Structural Paradigm · "to glare at / turn against," up the body
- 52/1 艮其趾 — "Glare at his feet"
- 52/2 艮其腓 — "…his calf"
- 52/3 艮其限 — "…his midsection"
- 52/4 艮其身 — "…his body"
- 52/5 艮其輔 — "…his cheeks" (Shaughnessy: the statement 艮其背 "glare at his back," misfiled as the hexagram statement, belongs at the 5th line.)
- 52/6 敦艮 — "Intensely glare."

## 漸 Jian (53) — Structural Paradigm · the wild goose advancing, low→high
- 53/1 鴻漸于干 — "The wild goose advances to the mountain stream"
- 53/2 鴻漸于磐 — "…to the large rock"
- 53/3 鴻漸于陸 — "…to the land" (clearest *xing*-evocation of marital separation: "the husband is on campaign but does not return; the wife is pregnant but does not give birth"; rhyme links 陸/復/育.)
- 53/4 鴻漸于木 — "…to the tree"
- 53/5 鴻漸于陵 — "…to the high mound"
- 53/6 鴻漸于阿 — "…to the hill" (emended; received text repeats 陸, breaking the progression).

## 歸妹 Guimei (54) — Developed Narrative · a marriage that fails for barrenness
- 54/1 歸妹以娣，跛能履，征吉 — "The marrying maiden with her younger sisters; the lame is able to walk; to campaign is auspicious."
- 54/2 眇能視，利幽人之貞 — "The blind is able to see; beneficial for a dark person's divination."
- 54/3 歸妹以須，反歸以娣 — "The marrying maiden with her elder sisters; returns with the younger sisters."
- 54/4 歸妹愆期，遲歸有時 — "The marrying maiden misses her time; she slowly returns to wait."
- 54/5 帝乙歸妹…其君之袂不如其娣之袂良；月幾望，吉 — "Di Yi marries off his daughter; the primary bride's sleeves are not as fine as the secondary's; nearly the full moon; auspicious." (The explicit historical reference — King Wen's marriage to a Shang princess.)
- 54/6 女承筐无實，士刲羊无血，无攸利 — "The lady holds the basket: no fruit; the man stabs the sheep: no blood; nothing beneficial." (Symbolic evocation of barrenness — the marriage's failure.)

## 既濟 Jiji (63) / 未濟 Weiji (64) — final pair · legitimating Zhou over Shang
- 63/3 高宗伐鬼方，三年克之，小人勿用 — "Gaozong [= Wu Ding, Shang's high tide] attacks the Guifang; in three years defeats them; the small man is not to be used."
- 63/5 東鄰殺牛，不如西鄰之禴祭，實受其福 — "The eastern neighbor [Shang] kills an ox, but not as good as the western neighbor's [Zhou] *yue*-sacrifice in really receiving its blessing."
- 64/4 震用伐鬼方，三年有賞于大國，貞吉，悔亡 — "Zhen [= Ji Li, Zhou's rise] attacks the Guifang; in three years rewarded by the great state; auspicious; problems gone."
- Shared identical lines across the pair (63/1↔64/1,64/2; 63/3↔64/4; 63/6↔64/6) mark the juxtaposition as a conscious editorial decision: Shang "Already Across" (finished), Zhou "Not Yet Across" (rising).
